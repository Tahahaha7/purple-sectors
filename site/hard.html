Article 1Scope

1.1

The model answers one question: in 2026 qualifying, how much of each lap time comes from the car, from the driver, from the track on the day, and from execution, and how certain is each answer.

1.2

It produces car pace by round, gaps between teammates, track evolution per circuit, the share of compromised laps, and a full posterior for every quantity. The Soft, Medium and Inter pages are views of this one posterior.

1.3

It does not model race pace, strategy, tyre degradation over a stint, or the order between drivers of different teams (see 7.1).

Article 2Data

2.1

Source: the public OpenF1 API. Main qualifying sessions only (Q1, Q2, Q3); Sprint Qualifying is excluded. Cancelled meetings are dropped and rounds use the official numbering.

2.2

The unit of data is a push lap: a timed lap that was not deleted, was not an out-lap, had no rain flag, and was within 7.0% of the fastest lap of its segment. The 7% cut sits in a natural gap between push laps and cool-down laps.

2.3

Each driver is kept only in their main car. A driver needs at least events after round ; substitutes below that are excluded.

2.4

The response is the log lap time, scaled so that every parameter reads as a percentage of lap time:

The site converts to seconds on a typical 90-second lap (1% = 0.9 s).

4.2

Prior predictive check. Before seeing lap times, 3,000 fake seasons were simulated on the real 2026 design and checked against limits set from F1 knowledge. Each implausible outcome now occurs in about 5% of simulated seasons or fewer: median field spread above 10% (5.4%), any event spread above 15% (2.3%), median teammate gap above 1.5% (0.1%), any driver worth more than 1.5% (3.0%), the track getting slower from Q1 to Q2 (5.8%).

4.3

Sensitivity. The informative prior puts the car share near 0.98 before any data. A second version (v1-B) with a neutral prior on the share (prior median 0.82) gives the same posterior: 0.985 against 0.983. The result comes from the data.

Article 5Inference

5.1

Stan (CmdStan 2.36 via CmdStanPy): 4 chains, 1,000 warm-up and 2,000 sampling iterations each, adapt_delta = 0.95.

5.2

Quality gate, applied to every fit before anything is published:

StatusR-hat (worst parameter)Divergent drawsEffect
pass≤ 1.01≤ 0.2%Published
warn≤ 1.05≤ 1%Published with a lower-confidence flag
fail> 1.05> 1%Blocked; the site keeps the previous round
5.3

Pipeline. Every Sunday at 06:00 UTC (retry on Monday), GitHub Actions fetches new sessions, refits, applies the gate, writes the site's data and redeploys. The history behind Medium and Inter is one fit per round using only the data available up to that round.

Article 7Limitations

7.1

Drivers are compared within teams only. With one car per driver, a pair's shared quality is indistinguishable from car pace. Cross-team driver rankings need drivers who changed teams, which a multi-season version (2023–2026) would add.

7.2

Head-to-head probabilities are conservative. Drivers adapt within a session (abort, go again, stop after a good lap), so a real best lap is tighter than "the best of several independent laps". The model overstates how uncertain best laps are, by about 20%.

7.3

Development and track fit trade off (resolved in v1.3). The data pin down their combined effect better than the split, so in v1.2 mixed slowly and three rounds passed the gate with a warning. v1.3 samples the total and the share instead (3.1–3.4): the effective sample size of rose 1.7–3.3× and round 14 went from R-hat 1.016 to 1.001, with identical estimates elsewhere.

7.4

Early rounds are uncertain by design. After one session the model has about 100 laps; its intervals are wide and honest, not wrong.

7.5

Wet sessions and laps flagged for rain are excluded rather than modelled.

Article 9Reproducibility

9.1

All code, data and model versions are public at github.com/Tahahaha7/purple-sectors, with the full development record in docs/model_log.md.

9.2
pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m purple_sectors.fetch --year 2026
python -m purple_sectors.fit --stan stan/quali_v13.stan --out posterior_v13.npz
python -m purple_sectors.export --posterior posterior_v13.npz --out site/data --snapshot-dir site/data/snapshots

Run with PYTHONPATH=src. The same data and seeds reproduce the published numbers to the third decimal.
