Article 1Scope

1.1

The model answers one question: in 2026 qualifying, how much of each lap time comes from the car, from the driver, from the track on the day, and from execution, and how certain is each answer.

1.2

It ranks drivers with the car removed and cars with the driver removed, and forecasts the next qualifying session (Soft); follows both rankings through the season (Medium); and shows, for any driver, where they would qualify in every car and how their battle with their teammate developed (Inter). Every page is a view of one posterior.

1.3

It does not model race pace, strategy, tyre degradation over a stint, or how long a driver would need to adapt to a different car.

Article 2Data

2.1

2026 laps. Every lap from main qualifying sessions (Q1, Q2, Q3), from the public OpenF1 API. Sprint qualifying is excluded; cancelled meetings are dropped and rounds use the official numbering.

2.2

The unit of 2026 data is a push lap: a timed lap that was not deleted, was not an out-lap, had no rain flag, and was within 7.0% of the fastest lap of its segment. Each driver is kept in their main car only; a driver needs at least events after round .

2.3

2022–25 results. Each driver's best Q1, Q2 and Q3 lap at every round of 2022–2025, from the Jolpica API (the successor of Ergast), plus every driver's debut season. Best laps beyond 107% of the segment's fastest (wet or aborted) are dropped. These seasons are background evidence only: nothing on the site is about them.

2.4

The response is the log lap time, scaled so that every parameter reads as a percentage of lap time:

The site converts to seconds on a typical 90-second lap (1% = 0.9 s).

This is what makes drivers comparable across teams. Within one season a driver's pace can only be compared with their teammate's, because a car and its two drivers always appear together. The history links every garage: drivers who moved between teams carry their measured skill with them.

3.6

Identification. Car pace sums to zero over the field at each event, so it reads against the average car. Driver skill is anchored by its history prior, not forced to average zero, and is shown on the site against the average driver on the grid:

The event baseline absorbs everything shared by all cars that day (weather, grip, the circuit), so conditions need no explicit model.

3.7

Counterfactuals. Driver in car has pace , computed draw by draw from the joint posterior and ranked against everyone else in their real seats. That is the "in every car" table on Inter, and it carries both the car's and the driver's uncertainty.

Article 4The history model (2022–25)

4.1

Why. Teammate comparisons alone cannot separate a fast driver in a slow car from a slow driver in a fast car. Over 2022–25, drivers moved between teams often enough to link every 2026 garage: through 65 teammate pairings, every driver on the 2026 grid is connected to every other.

4.2

Likelihood. Each best lap in season , round , segment , for driver in team :

A Student-t, because even a best lap can be compromised; the fitted says that happens often.

4.3

Cars. Every team has a separate car in every season: a random walk over that season's rounds plus a track fit, centred on each round's field. Nothing about a car carries from one season to the next.

4.4

Drivers. Skill carries over between seasons but can change, by more in a driver's second and third seasons, when young drivers improve fastest. Skill is centred on each season's grid.

Fitted: s per season for established drivers and about twice that, s, for young ones. Driver skill is stable enough to be strong evidence.

4.5

The 2026 prior. Each driver's posterior skill in their last season, plus one season of possible change (wider for drivers entering their second or third season, and wider again for each season out of F1), becomes in Article 3.5. A rookie with no history gets the population prior , so 2026 alone decides.

5.2

Prior predictive check. Before seeing lap times, 3,000 fake seasons were simulated on the real 2026 design and checked against limits set from F1 knowledge. Each implausible outcome occurs in about 5% of simulated seasons or fewer: median field spread above 10% (5.4%), any event spread above 15% (2.3%), median teammate gap above 1.5% (0.1%), any driver worth more than 1.5% (3.0%), the track getting slower from Q1 to Q2 (5.8%).

Article 6Inference

6.1

Stan (CmdStan 2.36 via CmdStanPy): 4 chains, 1,000 warm-up and 2,000 sampling iterations each, adapt_delta = 0.95 (0.99 when a fit has three rounds or fewer).

6.2

Quality gate, applied to every 2026 fit before anything is published:

StatusR-hat (worst parameter)Divergent drawsEffect
pass≤ 1.01≤ 0.2%Published
warn≤ 1.05≤ 1%Published with a lower-confidence flag
fail> 1.05> 1%Blocked; the site keeps the previous round
6.3

Pipeline. Every Sunday at 06:00 UTC (retry on Monday), GitHub Actions runs the tests, fetches new sessions, refits v2.0, applies the gate, writes the site's data, forecasts the next round and redeploys. The history model runs once per season, or whenever an earlier season's results change, and its driver priors are committed to the repository. The season history behind Medium is one fit per round using only the data available up to that round.

Article 8Limitations

8.1

Cross-team comparisons rest on three assumptions. A driver's skill is the same in any car (no car–driver fit), it changes only gradually between seasons (faster in a driver's second and third seasons), and best laps from 2022–25 measure it fairly. Comparisons that run through long chains of teammates are less certain, and the intervals show it.

8.2

Counterfactuals ignore adaptation. "In every car" assumes the driver is immediately at home in that car.

8.3

Teammate-battle and forecast probabilities are cautious. Drivers adapt within a session (abort, go again, stop after a good lap), so a real best lap is tighter than "the best of several independent laps". The model overstates how uncertain best laps are, by about 20%.

8.4

Rookies. A driver with no earlier season (in 2026, Lindblad) is learned from 2026 alone, so his comparisons across teams are the least certain on the grid.

8.5

Early rounds are uncertain by design, and wet sessions and rain-flagged laps are excluded rather than modelled.

Article 10Reproducibility

10.1

All code, data and model versions are public at github.com/Tahahaha7/purple-sectors, with the full development record in docs/model_log.md.

10.2
pip install -r requirements.txt
export PYTHONPATH=src
python -m purple_sectors.history --from 2022 --to 2025          # 2022-25 results, debuts, network
python -m purple_sectors.history_fit --last 2025 --priors data/priors/drivers_2026.json
python -m purple_sectors.fetch --year 2026
python -m purple_sectors.fit --stan stan/quali_v20.stan --out posterior_v20.npz
python -m purple_sectors.export --posterior posterior_v20.npz --out site/data --snapshot-dir site/data/snapshots

The same data and seeds reproduce the published numbers to the third decimal.
