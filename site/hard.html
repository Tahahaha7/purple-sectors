Article 1Scope

1.1

The model answers one question about every qualifying push lap since 2023: how much of the lap time comes from the car, how much from the driver, how much from the match between the two, how much from the track on the day, and how much from execution, and how certain each answer is.

1.2

From that one posterior the site ranks drivers with the car removed and cars with the driver removed, puts every driver in every car, forecasts the next qualifying session (Soft); follows the season round by round (Medium); and profiles any driver, with their teammate battle (Inter).

1.3

It does not model race pace, strategy, tyre degradation over a stint, or how long a driver would need to adapt to a different car.

Article 2Data

2.1

Laps, 2023 onward. Every lap from main qualifying sessions (Q1, Q2, Q3) from the public OpenF1 API, which starts in 2023. Sprint qualifying is excluded; cancelled meetings are dropped and rounds use the official numbering.

2.2

The unit of data is a push lap: a timed lap that was not an out-lap, had no rain flag, and was within 7.0% of the fastest lap of its segment (the 107% rule). Laps deleted for track limits are kept: the car did go that fast. Every driver and every lap is in the model; the pages show drivers with at least events after round .

2.3

Debuts and teammates. Each driver's first F1 season and every teammate pairing since 2022 come from the Jolpica API (the successor of Ergast). The debut tells the model who is still learning; the pairings draw the network on Inter.

2.4

The response is the log lap time, scaled so that every parameter reads as a percentage of lap time:

The site converts to seconds on a typical 90-second lap (1% = 0.9 s).

Article 3The model

3.1

Likelihood. Each lap is either clean or compromised (traffic, a mistake, a yellow flag). Both are exponentially modified Gaussians: noise around the lap's potential, plus a loss that can only cost time.

3.2

Lap potential. For a lap by driver in car (a team in one season) at event of season , segment , starting (tens of minutes from the segment's mean lap start):

One baseline per event and segment absorbs everything shared by the whole field that day (the circuit, the weather, the step from Q1 to Q2 to Q3); a slope per event follows the track rubbering in within a segment.

3.3

Car. A team in one season is one car. Its pace is a persistent part , a random walk over the season's rounds with steps scaled by the weeks between them, plus a one-off deviation at each round. The round-to-round movement is sampled as a total and the share of it that persists, which the data separate far better than the two scales on their own. Both parts are centred on the field at every round, so car pace always reads against the average car.

The rankings and the counterfactual grid use the persistent part at the latest round; the per-round value is what the car did at that particular track.

3.4

Driver. One skill per driver per season, relative to that season's grid. It carries over between seasons as a random walk, starting from a rookie offset in the debut season, with a mean change and extra movement in a driver's second and third seasons, when young drivers change fastest:

3.5

Fit. One term for every driver-and-car pair that happened, shrunk towards zero by a scale the data decide. It is what lets the car and the driver be told apart honestly: a driver who never changed team has skill and fit entangled, and the interval says so; a driver who moved pins both down. Teammate battles are compared in their shared car, so they include it.

3.6

Counterfactuals. Driver in car has pace , computed draw by draw from the joint posterior and ranked against everyone else in their real seats. When the pair has happened, is its posterior; when it has not, it is a draw from the prior, so the interval widens and the square on Soft is drawn dashed.

Article 4Across teams and seasons

4.1

Why one model. Within one season a car and its two drivers always appear together, so teammate comparisons alone cannot separate a fast driver in a slow car from a slow driver in a fast car. Drivers who changed team carry their skill with them, and because every season since 2023 is in the same fit, every garage is linked to every other through those moves. Earlier designs did this in two stages, a history model whose summary became the prior of a season model; that lost the correlations between drivers and defined skill on best laps in one place and on all laps in the other. Here there is one posterior and one definition.

4.2

Identification. Car pace sums to zero over the field at each round and driver skill over each season's grid, so both read against the average. The baselines carry the absolute level. The strength of the cross-team link is governed by how much skill is allowed to drift between seasons; the drift scale is estimated but weakly informed, and its prior is listed in Article 5 for that reason.

4.3

Who is comparable. Drivers outside the main teammate network are only comparable with their own teammates. The network and its components are shown on Inter and written to the fit report.

5.2

Prior predictive check. Before any lap time is seen, seasons are simulated from the priors on the real design (the notebook's prior check) and compared with limits set from F1 knowledge: car spreads, teammate gaps, how much time a bad lap loses. Implausible outcomes should be rare under the prior, not impossible.

Article 6Inference

6.1

Stan (CmdStan 2.36 via CmdStanPy): 4 chains, 1,000 warm-up and 1,000 sampling iterations each, adapt_delta = 0.95.

6.2

Quality gate, applied to every fit before anything is published:

A fit that does not pass is run once more with a more careful sampler (adapt_delta = 0.99, longer warm-up) before the verdict. No saturated tree depths are allowed for a pass.

6.3

Pipeline. Every Sunday at 06:00 UTC (retry on Monday), GitHub Actions runs the tests, fetches new sessions, refits every season since 2023 in one go, applies the gate, writes the site's data, forecasts the next round and redeploys. The season history behind Medium is one fit per round using only the data available up to that round, with today's model: a replay, not what was published at the time.

Article 7Validation

7.1

Parameter recovery. A history is simulated on the real design from known parameters and fitted; the share of true values inside the 90% intervals should be around 90% for driver skill, car pace, fit and the hyperparameters. The check runs from the notebook and from the fit script (--recovery), and its numbers are written to the fit report.

7.3

Posterior predictive checks. The model reproduces the shape of lap times (skewness and kurtosis). Statistics built from each driver's best lap (teammate gap, field spread, Q1→Q2 step) fall in the tails; see 8.3.

7.4

Still to do. A rolling-origin hold-out (fit through 2023, predict 2024; through 2024, predict 2025; through 2025, predict 2026), scoring the sign, the Brier score and the coverage of teammate gaps, rookies included; and a best-lap calibration of head-to-head probabilities.

Article 8Limitations

8.1

Cross-team comparisons rest on two assumptions. Skill changes only gradually between seasons (faster in a driver's second and third seasons), and the fit between a driver and a car is a modest, shrunk adjustment rather than a different driver. The 2026 regulations are a reset; a driver whose strengths suit the new cars better than the old will move further from last season than the drift expects, and the intervals are the honest statement of that.

8.2

Counterfactuals ignore adaptation. "In every car" assumes the driver is immediately at home in that car, and for pairs that never happened the fit is a guess from the prior.

8.3

Teammate-battle and forecast probabilities are cautious. Drivers adapt within a session (abort, go again, stop after a good lap), so a real best lap is tighter than "the best of several independent laps". The model overstates how uncertain best laps are, by about 20%.

8.4

Rookies. A driver with no earlier season starts from the rookie offset and is otherwise learned from this season alone, so his comparisons across teams are the least certain on the grid.

8.5

Early rounds are uncertain by design, and wet sessions and rain-flagged laps are excluded rather than modelled.

Article 10Reproducibility

10.1

All code, data and the model are public at github.com/Tahahaha7/purple-sectors; the model and its limits are written up in docs/pace_model.md.

10.2
pip install -r requirements.txt
export PYTHONPATH=src
for y in 2023 2024 2025 2026; do python -m purple_sectors.fetch --year $y; done
python -m purple_sectors.history --from 2022 --to 2025                      # debuts and the teammate network
python -m purple_sectors.pace.fit --years 2023 2024 2025 2026 --out posterior_pace.npz --report fit.json --retry
python -m purple_sectors.pace.export --posterior posterior_pace.npz --laps data/raw/quali_laps_2026.csv \
    --out site/data --snapshot-dir site/data/snapshots

The same data and seeds reproduce the published numbers to the third decimal.
